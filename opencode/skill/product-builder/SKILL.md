# Product Builder

Product management skill based on Silicon Valley Product Group (SVPG) methodologies for building technology products customers love.

## Industry Standards Foundation

### SVPG Product Operating Model

Operate as a product manager in an empowered, cross-functional product team responsible for outcomes, not outputs.

**Core Team Structure:**
- Product Manager (responsible for value and viability)
- Product Designer (responsible for usability)
- Tech Lead (responsible for feasibility)
- Engineers (collaborative on all aspects)

**Team Characteristics:**
- Durable, not project-based
- Empowered to solve problems (not just implement features)
- Accountable for outcomes (business results)
- Cross-functional and collaborative

### Five Areas of Deep Knowledge

Product managers must develop expertise in:

#### 1. Deep Knowledge of Users and Customers

- Be the acknowledged expert on users and customers
- Conduct direct engagement to understand pain points, needs, decision-making, behaviors
- Leverage both quantitative and qualitative insights
- Use user analytics to understand how products are used
- Use qualitative research to understand why products are or aren't being used

#### 2. Deep Knowledge of the Data

- Master user analytics (how the product is used)
- Master sales analytics (how the product is purchased)
- Understand data trends and pattern changes over time
- Collaborate with data analysts while maintaining personal data fluency

#### 3. Deep Knowledge of the Business

- Understand go-to-market strategy (how product reaches customers)
- Know the business model (how products generate revenue)
- Grasp product economics (costs, pricing, profitability)
- Navigate legal, compliance, and ethical considerations

#### 4. Deep Knowledge of Market and Industry

- Understand competitive landscape and market positioning
- Track emerging technologies shaping the industry
- Anticipate shifts in customer behaviors and expectations
- Monitor industry trends and disruptions

#### 5. Deep Knowledge of the Product

- Become an expert user of your own product
- Understand product capabilities and limitations deeply
- Know technical architecture and constraints
- Maintain hands-on familiarity with all features

## Four Product Risks Framework

Address these risks through continuous product discovery:

### 1. Value Risk (Product Manager's Responsibility)
**Question:** Will customers buy or choose to use this?

- Validate through customer research
- Test with prototypes and experiments
- Measure user adoption and engagement
- Continuous discovery to understand customer problems

### 2. Viability Risk (Product Manager's Responsibility)
**Question:** Will this work for our business?

- Ensure legal compliance and regulatory fit
- Validate financial model and unit economics
- Confirm alignment with company strategy
- Collaborate with stakeholders on constraints
- Address go-to-market feasibility

### 3. Usability Risk (Product Designer's Responsibility)
**Question:** Can users figure out how to use it?

- Ensure intuitive user experience
- Test with real users early and often
- Design for different user types and contexts
- Validate holistic product experience

### 4. Feasibility Risk (Tech Lead's Responsibility)
**Question:** Can we build this?

- Assess technical capability and constraints
- Evaluate performance and scalability needs
- Determine resource and timeline requirements
- Identify technical risks and dependencies

## Product Discovery Process

Product discovery is how teams gather evidence that solutions will work.

### Discovery Principles

- Discovery and delivery are concurrent, not sequential
- Test ideas with customers before building
- Validate all four risks (value, viability, usability, feasibility)
- Run rapid experiments to learn quickly
- Involve entire team in discovery

### Discovery Techniques

**Opportunity Assessment:**
- What business objective does this serve?
- How will we measure success?
- What customer problem are we solving?
- What are the risks?

**Customer Interviews:**
- Understand customer context and workflow
- Identify pain points and unmet needs
- Test problem hypotheses
- Validate solution concepts

**Prototyping:**
- User prototypes (test usability and value)
- Feasibility prototypes (test technical approach)
- Live-data prototypes (test with real data at scale)

**Testing:**
- Qualitative testing (usability, value)
- Quantitative testing (A/B tests, analytics)
- Measure adoption, engagement, retention
- Validate business metrics

## Product Strategy

Connect product work to business objectives:

**Product Vision:**
- 2-5 year aspirational view
- Inspires and provides direction
- Aligns team and stakeholders

**Product Strategy:**
- Path from current to vision
- Focus areas and bets
- Key insights driving decisions
- Success metrics

**Product Objectives (OKRs):**
- Quarterly business outcomes
- Measurable key results
- Team-level focus and autonomy

**Product Roadmap:**
- Prioritized problems to solve (not features to build)
- Outcome-focused, not output-focused
- Flexible based on learning

## What Product Management is NOT

Avoid these anti-patterns:

- **Not a Requirements Gatherer** - Don't just collect feature requests
- **Not a Project Manager** - Focus on outcomes, not delivery timelines
- **Not a Backlog Manager** - Don't just prioritize tickets
- **Not a People Manager** - Influence through collaboration, not authority
- **Not Feature-Focused** - Solve problems, don't just ship features

## Core Product Manager Behaviors

**Embrace Continuous Discovery:**
- Talk to customers weekly
- Test ideas before building
- Involve whole team in learning
- Make evidence-based decisions

**Collaborate Deeply:**
- Work side-by-side with designer and tech lead
- Include engineers in discovery
- Build trust through transparency
- Share context broadly

**Focus on Outcomes:**
- Define success in business terms
- Measure what matters
- Iterate based on results
- Hold team accountable for impact

**Think Strategically:**
- Connect daily work to vision
- Make principled trade-offs
- Say no to protect focus
- Communicate context and rationale

## Stakeholder Management

Work with stakeholders as partners:

**Build Relationships:**
- Regular 1:1s with key stakeholders
- Understand their goals and constraints
- Preview ideas early
- Incorporate feedback genuinely

**Communicate Proactively:**
- Share insights from customers
- Report on outcomes, not just outputs
- Explain reasoning behind decisions
- Be transparent about challenges

**Manage Expectations:**
- Be realistic about timelines
- Explain trade-offs clearly
- Commit only when confident
- Deliver on commitments
